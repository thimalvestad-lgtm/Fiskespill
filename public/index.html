    Day 1

    Riverbend

    A quiet stretch of trout stream: a fast riffle, a garden of boulders and a long, glassy flat. Read the water, watch for rising fish, choose your rig and cast.

    How to play
    • Look around by dragging; zoom with the mouse wheel or 🔍. Tap a circle on the map to walk to another part of the bank.
    • Spot fish: tap a dark shape or a flash under the water, or a rise ring, for points. Spotted fish stay marked. Polarized glasses (🕶) cut the glare.
    • Aim by tapping the water. Land the fly a little upstream of a fish and let the current carry it over. A fly landing right on its head, or line falling across it, spooks the fish.
    • Rig up: pick a fly from the Fly box, and build your Leader: a tapered leader, a tippet section, and the indicator depth or dropper length to suit the water.
    • While the fly drifts, Mend flips the line upstream so it doesn't drag the fly.
    • When a fish takes, hit Strike! Then hold Reel and use the drag buttons to tighten or loosen.
    • The better your rig suits the water and the conditions, the more often fish take, and the bigger they are.

    Best with sound on. Headphones make the river sound lovely.

      Field guide

      Reading the river

      Where trout live

      A trout wants three things: shelter from the current, safety from predators, and food delivered to its door. So look for seams where fast water meets slow, pockets behind boulders, drop-offs below riffles, the deep head of a pool, and dark undercut banks. The best lies hold the biggest fish.

      Flat water and tailouts hold fish mostly when insects are hatching, or in low light at dawn and dusk.

      Spotting fish

      Trout are easiest to see in slow, clear water with the sun high. Look for a shadow on the bottom, or a tail that moves. Broken water and coloured water hide them. When fish are rising, the rings give them away.

      Presenting the fly

      Cast a metre or two upstream of a fish and let the fly drift down to it. A fly that slaps down on a fish's head, or line that falls across it, sends it bolting. On the flat, a long leader keeps the heavy fly line further from the fish.

      Leader & tippet

      In clear, slow water, use a long leader (12 ft or more) and fine tippet (5X–6X). In fast, broken water, a shorter leader gives control, and a stronger tippet (3X–4X) lets you set the drag tight to stop a fish reaching the rocks. Coloured water forgives a thicker tippet.

      Choosing a fly

      Rising fish? Use a dry fly about the size of the insects you see. Nothing on the surface? Use a nymph: heavier in deep or fast water, smaller on the flat. The dry dropper fishes both depths and suits riffles, seams and banks.

      Playing a fish

      Set the drag a bit below what your tippet can take. When the fish runs, let the reel give line; when it rests or swims towards you, reel. If the fish heads for cover, a tighter drag (with strong enough tippet) turns it.
