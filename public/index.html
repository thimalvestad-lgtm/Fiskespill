    Riverbend

    A quiet stretch of trout stream: a fast riffle, a garden of boulders and a long, glassy flat. Read the water, choose your rig, and see what rises.

    How to play
    • Tap a ring on the river to walk to a spot. Each one is a different kind of water.
    • Build your rig: leader length, tippet thickness, fly type and size.
    • Cast, then watch the fly drift. When a fish takes, tap Strike!
    • Hold to reel in. Ease off when the fish runs, or the tippet will break. In rocky water, though, a fish given too much line will wrap you round a rock.
    • The better your rig suits the water and the conditions, the more often fish take, and the bigger they are.

    Best with sound on. Headphones make the river sound lovely.

      Field guide

      Reading the river

      Where trout live

      A trout wants three things: shelter from the current, safety from predators, and food delivered to its door. So look for seams where fast water meets slow, pockets behind boulders, drop-offs below riffles, the deep head of a pool, and dark undercut banks. The best lies hold the biggest fish.

      Flat water and tailouts hold fish mostly when insects are hatching, or in low light at dawn and dusk.

      Leader & tippet

      In clear, slow water, fish see everything: use a long leader (12 ft or more) and fine tippet (5X–6X). In fast, broken water, a shorter leader gives you control, and a stronger tippet (3X–4X) can stop a fish before it reaches the rocks. Coloured water forgives a thicker tippet. Bright sun makes fish warier; overcast and rain make them bolder.

      Choosing a fly

      Rising fish? Use a dry fly about the size of the insects you see. Nothing on the surface? Most feeding happens near the bottom, so use a nymph. Go heavier in deep or fast water and smaller on the flat. The dry dropper covers both depths and suits riffles, seams and banks, especially when you're not sure.

      A day on the river

      Dawn and dusk are prime time, and the big browns come out of hiding. Midday sun pushes fish into deep or broken water. Watch the river notes: they tell you what's hatching, and when.
